# Lanternmere: first-year story expansion

Status: **proposed production plan**, not a claim that this material is written or playable. The current game has eight compact main chapters, three spells, 37 destinations, 45 profiles and introductions, fuller conversations for the central trio and 12 supporting characters, eight rotating gatherings, and six holiday activities. This document expands that foundation; it does not import canon or instructions from the abandoned project's README or JSON sidecars.

The objective is a substantial, fully authored offline novel-game built around the supplied art. AI can add optional conversation, but must not supply required chapters, clues, relationship milestones, or word-count credit.

**Cast expansion:** the original 45 identities are a foundation, not a limit. [CAST_EXPANSION.md](CAST_EXPANSION.md) maps the wider academy and proposes 15 new people for missing teaching roles, dorm companionship, complete house sports squads, village trades, and further romance candidates. Its coverage plan extends the original cast table below. New characters need their own scenes and artwork; none are implemented merely by appearing in this plan.

## The promise of the year

You arrive at an adult academy on an island in a lake. The lantern above your seat goes out. With Rowan, Imogen, and Saoirse, you discover that the school's welcoming magic depends on the ordinary people its records have gradually forgotten. Recovering names is the beginning: the living people whose stories you find must have a say in how those stories are kept. At midsummer, the academy must restore a circle of willing keepers before the Heartfire loses its welcoming magic.

The threat remains the one already established in the game: the buildings survive if the light fails, but the magic of belonging does not. There is no need to inflate the stakes into an apocalypse. Darkening corridors, disrupted crossings, exhausted carers, damaged friendships, and unfair institutional decisions make that loss concrete.

The conflict comes from competing good intentions and mistakes with consequences. Hask inherited a register that records honours more readily than care. Honoria delayed acknowledging the problem because she feared losing the school. Rowan tries to do too much alone; Imogen can confuse preserving a story with owning it; Saoirse wants a device to solve a problem that also requires people. Their positions change through specific decisions and repair, rather than a final speech making every disagreement disappear.

## Length and the unit of play

Plan for **270,000 unique authored player-facing words across 172 scene chapters**. The release floor is 250,000 genuinely distinct authored words after revisions and duplicate removal. A scene chapter is one continuous dramatic situation, usually in one environment, with its own intention, complication, meaningful choice, and changed situation at the end. A change of subject in a repeatable topic menu does not count as another chapter.

| Content strand | Scene chapters | Average words per chapter | Planned unique words |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Main first-year story: eight acts, eight chapters each | 64 | 2,250 | 144,000 |
| Rowan, Imogen, Saoirse personal arcs: six each | 18 | 2,000 | 36,000 |
| Eight supporting arcs: three each | 24 | 1,500 | 36,000 |
| Six community quest pairs | 12 | 1,000 | 12,000 |
| Four house stories: three chapters each | 12 | 1,000 | 12,000 |
| Six optional romance routes: three additional chapters each | 18 | 750 | 13,500 |
| Six equally developed friendship alternatives: three each | 18 | 750 | 13,500 |
| Six ending/epilogue chapters | 6 | 500 | 3,000 |
| **Total** | **172** | | **270,000** |

These are editorial budgets, not instructions to stretch a scene. Move words to a scene with unresolved dramatic work when another earns its ending sooner. Main chapters can range around 2,000–4,000 words; a brief transition should remain brief. Split a long sequence when its location, immediate goal, or principal conflict changes.

Word counts include authored narration, dialogue, substantive choice wording, and distinct outcomes. They exclude code, interface labels, biographies repeated elsewhere, placeholders, development notes, duplicated shared passages, and generated AI replies. A shared scene referenced by both modes is counted once. Branch-exclusive prose counts toward the corpus but only toward a reading path that actually encounters it.

**Corpus length and reading length must be reported separately.** The 250,000-word minimum applies to unique playable content across the shipping game. A streamlined main-story run will be shorter than the full corpus. The gross allocation for a friendship-focused run through the main story, personal and community quests, one house, friendship alternatives, and one ending is approximately 245,000 words before exclusive choices are removed. This is a planning allocation, not a measured playthrough. Measure the shortest complete story, a representative optional-romance run, and the longest reachable single-save run once chapters exist. Do not present incompatible romance/friendship alternatives or six different endings as one read-through.

## Interleaved quests and houses

The six community pairs each contain a setup chapter and a later consequence chapter: **A supper that stays warm** (Moll/Bassani/Bram); **A memory is not a measure** (Delphine/Kovac/Maisie); **A building made by hands** (Grey/Arkwright/Hester); **The song with room for another voice** (Kit/Hamish/Lettice); **A route worth sharing** (Dev/Jory/Ombree); **A book that can be corrected** (Mina/Hask/Dunne/Corliss). The principal cast member leads each scene; the others appear when their expertise or disagreement matters. Do not put everyone in a conversation to satisfy an appearance quota.

Each house has three scenes: a local problem in autumn, a contested response in winter, and a contribution in spring. Owlcombe must learn when to stop investigating and ask permission. Rookhallow must distinguish courage from unsafe persistence. Heronmere must share care without expecting one person to provide it all. Larkspire must finish a useful experiment rather than abandon it for a more exciting one. These are different conflicts, not four reskins of the same quiz. The player's chosen house has the full local route; other houses' contributions remain understandable in the main story.

Quest progression follows the year but does not require daily attendance or an exact calendar date. Offer important scenes in a chapter window; move unresolved essentials into a clearly labelled catch-up appointment or retrospective scene before advancing the act. Cosmetic gatherings can be missed. Essential clues, taught spells, and the complete ending cannot be lost to a daily timer, low points, an unpopular opinion, or refusal of romance. Repeated daily dialogue remains companionship, not a requirement to grind through the plot.

At act boundaries, Book mode offers a short curated table of eligible personal and community chapters. Live mode offers the same scene IDs at their locations, with directions and appointments. Both modes can complete every essential step offline. Side stories alter roles, wording, practical solutions, decorations, records, or epilogues; none may hold the only copy of a required clue.

## Optional romance and friendship

Proposed canon: Lanternmere is an adult further-education academy and the player is **18 or older**. The six initial proposed romance candidates are adult peers: Rowan (21), Imogen (22), Saoirse (20), Cas (22), Noor (21), and Idris (29). Cast expansion adds Soren (24) and Iona (23) as further candidates, with room for other distinct routes. These ages are proposed writing facts, not ages inferred from portraits. Record them in the implemented character data before romance is enabled; review any portrait that appears inconsistent rather than silently assuming an age. Faculty, healers, evaluators, and other characters with authority over the player are not romance routes. The budget currently allocates six routes; revise its scene and word allocation when commissioning additional full routes, including friendship alternatives.

Each candidate has three additional intimacy chapters and three substantial friendship alternatives. The extra scenes explore an individual tension: Rowan's right to receive care, Imogen's wish to be known without being fully explained, Saoirse enjoying time without a project, Cas allowing an imperfect shared plan, Noor balancing two homes, and Idris making room for a new beginning without erasing an earlier life. Settings stay within existing art: common rooms, the library, glasshouses, rookery, village, and lakeshore.

The player explicitly chooses romantic or platonic interest; affection points never automatically create a romance. A declined invitation preserves the friendship and its quest rewards. Use non-explicit tenderness, a date, a permitted hand-hold or kiss, an honest boundary, and a future plan. Friendship alternatives receive equal narrative investment and equivalent practical benefits. Do not make romance the price of an exclusive spell, a necessary confession, house success, or the good ending. For the first release, one active romantic route at a time keeps continuity manageable; changing course is an authored respectful conversation, not a hidden penalty.

## Authoring contract for each scene

Use stable IDs and structured beats so text, portrait direction, choices, audio, and saved progress refer to the same content. The intended contract is:

```js
{
  id: 'year1.act4.wrong-name',
  act: 4,
  strand: 'main',
  location: 'lantern-hall',
  background: 'lantern-hall-emberfall',
  window: { after: ['year1.act4.paper-promises'], before: ['year1.act4.correction'] },
  objective: 'Check whose memory the lantern actually holds',
  participants: ['imogen', 'noor'],
  beats: [
    // narration | spoken line | choice | authored transition
    // Every line has a stable ID; spoken lines have a speaker and expression.
    // Staging gives left/right position, entrance, listening presence, and exit.
    // Expression IDs must exist in the asset manifest or use an explicit reviewed fallback.
  ],
  choices: [], // visible consequences + local flags; explicit rejoin points
  onComplete: [], // validated deterministic changes, not arbitrary code or AI instructions
  journal: { summary: '', evidence: [] },
  callbacks: [],
  assetsRequired: [],
  editorial: { draftState: 'outline', uniqueWordCount: 0, readingPathWords: {} }
}
```

Most beats should fit one comfortable text box; split at natural turns, not by arbitrary character count. Expressions follow the intent of that line and the response to the previous one. A pause or narrated action can justify a change; the next sentence does not need a different face merely because it is next. A listener remains visible only while present in the scene. Essential meaning must remain in text when animation is disabled.

Every finished scene must establish what somebody wants, place something in the way, make at least one choice or revealing action matter, and leave evidence of change. Recurring scenes need new information, a changed relationship, a new practical constraint, or a payoff. A diary recap, seasonal adjective swap, or repeated moral is not sufficient justification for another 1,500 words.

Track the source of each clue and what each speaker currently knows. Give lies, errors, guesses, and memories explicit narrative status. Track unresolved promises and pay them off or acknowledge them in an epilogue. Characters should be recognisable without their name labels: different syntax, humour, priorities, ways of disagreeing, and subjects they do not know about.

## Production sequence and acceptance

1. **Measure the present build.** Count shipped prose, unique scene units, branch text, expression coverage, and reachable routes. Store the report with the content version so the proposed budget cannot be mistaken for delivered words.
2. **Build one complete vertical slice.** Expand the arrival through the first Kindle lesson into approximately 10,000–15,000 polished words using the expressive trio and existing backgrounds. Include a two-person exchange, an expression change on a specific line, a meaningful rejoin, a saved mid-scene position, and a Book/Live entry to the same chapter. Test reading pace before committing to the rest.
3. **Lock the year's cause-and-effect outline.** Specify each act's clue, choice, follow-up, and failure recovery; establish adult peer canon; establish the two ending policies. Do not commission a new environment for an unused outline.
4. **Write the main arc in act packages.** Each package includes scenes, consequences, callbacks, journal entries, portrait directions, and automated graph validation. The draft is not complete until its playable consequences exist. Prioritise the earliest chapters so readers receive a coherent sequence as it grows.
5. **Interleave the trio, then supporting and community arcs.** A quest must answer a need arising in the main story or the character's own goal. Commission secondary expressions from approved scenes. Add house routes and equal friendship alternatives before treating romance as finished.
6. **Reach the word target through earned scenes.** Track planned, drafted, edited, integrated, and tested words separately. Do not count an outline or a prose file that cannot be reached in the application as implemented content. If the corpus or complete reading path is short, identify missing conflict or payoff before adding material.
7. **Finish and verify the year.** Test every branch for a reachable ending, every promise for a callback, every date jump for caught-up essentials, and every required asset for offline availability. Run a no-romance friendship route, a low-points route, a representative romance route, and a Book-mode route with network disabled. Long saves must resume at a stable beat ID and survive content updates with a migration.

Release acceptance includes an audited corpus of at least 250,000 distinct words; measured offline reading paths reported separately from that corpus; the current total and path range visible in project documentation; purposeful use of all 45 characters; implemented outcomes for authored choices; and a complete, achievable climax in both modes. The 270,000-word plan is a budget with room for cuts, not a progress report.

Art requests should cite the approved scene IDs that need them. The highest-value additions are reviewed expressions for recurring peers, spring/summer variants of existing central spaces, and a small number of ending-specific states. Preserve supplied compositions and identities. A substantial game can grow through these places becoming meaningful over time; it does not require inventing dozens of new destinations before the existing 37 have stories.
